Sensitivity ranking: which number you notice most

Quick answer

Not all numbers carry the same weight. By their typical change range, the ones that move the feel the most are the head tube angle (via the trail) and the reach; right behind, the effective seat tube angle (climbing) and the bottom bracket drop. The stack and the offset are usually finer nuances. It is a guide to prioritize your attention, not a law: real sensitivity depends on you, your terrain and how the numbers interact.

When you choose or adjust a bike you don't have time to wrestle with all twelve numbers at once. This tells you where to spend your attention and where to relax.

The rookie mistake is to obsess over a minor figure (the offset, one mm of bottom bracket) and ignore the one that truly defines the bike (the reach, the angle). This ranking puts each thing in its place.

Tier 1 · Hugely noticeable

The first thing that defines the bike
Head tube angle (~1°)
Changes the entire character: composure on descents vs agility. It acts via the trail, which also drags front center and wheelbase. The one that most defines how the bike "feels" at speed.
Reach (~10–15 mm)
Decides whether the bike fits you and how you ride standing on descents. A few mm outside your range are felt in position, control and confidence. It matters more than the S/M/L size.

Tier 2 · Clearly noticeable

Defines how it climbs and how it plants
Effective seat tube angle (~1°)
Rules the seated climb: weight, efficiency and whether the front wheel lifts. Very sensitive because it also changes with your saddle height.
Bottom bracket drop (a few mm)
A few millimeters change the composure in corners and the frequency of pedal strikes. Pure center of gravity.
Wheelbase (~20 mm)
Straight and calm vs agile and maneuverable. Noticed above all when comparing opposite terrains (high speed vs tight corner).

Tier 3 · Finer nuance

Fine-tune around the above
Chainstay length (~5–10 mm)
Changes the balance and the manuals, but its effect is more subtle and very dependent on which front center it is combined with.
Stack (~10–20 mm)
Very felt in comfort and position, less in "performance". Plus it is easily adjusted with spacers and bars.
Fork offset (a few mm)
Fine-tunes the weight of the steering and the wheel flop at low speed. Real, but the finest of all for the average rider.
How to use it: when buying, get Tier 1 right first (reach + angle) — they are the ones you can't fix with parts. Tier 2 you choose with the size and the model; Tier 3 is fine-tuned afterward with setup and components. And avoid the common mistakes reading the chart.
Honesty notice: this ranking is qualitative, not a measurement. The order changes by discipline (in XC the seat angle and the climb weigh more; in DH, the head tube angle and the wheelbase), by your level and by how the numbers interact with each other. Take it as a map of priorities, not as an exact table. For the detailed "what I feel" of each one, see the number-to-feel table.

FAQ

What is noticed most?

The head tube angle (via trail) and the reach. Right behind, the effective seat tube angle and the bottom bracket drop.

Is the ranking reliable?

It is a qualitative guide to prioritize, not a law. It changes by discipline, level and interactions between numbers.

What do I look at when buying?

The reach and the head tube angle: they are the ones you can't fix with parts and define whether the bike will fit you well.
