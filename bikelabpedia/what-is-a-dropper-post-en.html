What is a dropper post?

Quick answer

A dropper post is a seatpost that raises and lowers the saddle on the fly from a remote, without stopping or getting off the bike. Actuation can be cable, hydraulic or wireless, and an internal cartridge (air or IFP) returns it to the up position. Typical travels run from 100 to 240 mm: saddle down on descents (low centre of gravity and freedom of movement), up to pedal efficiently. If it sounds right, the next step is to see what dropper post fits your bike; and if you already have one and it won't respond, check why the dropper won't go up.

It's the part that changes the feel on descents the most without touching your suspension. Here's what it does and when it matters.

What it does, in one sentence

The dropper replaces the fixed seatpost. Instead of stopping, opening the collar and moving the saddle by hand, you press a remote on the handlebar and the saddle drops under your weight; release the remote and an internal cartridge raises it again. All on the move, without stopping.

How it works inside

Actuation. The remote can pull a cable, send pressure down a hydraulic line, or be wireless. It's the part you open and close.

Return. Inside there's a cartridge that stores the energy to rise again: in some it's pressurised air, in others an internal floating piston (IFP). That cartridge is what pushes the saddle back up when you release the remote.

What it's for

Saddle down on descents: the centre of gravity drops and you gain freedom of movement on the bike, which means more control on technical, steep terrain. Saddle up to pedal: it sits at its efficient height, the usual one. The point is switching from one to the other without stopping.

What it pairs with

If this interests you, the next step isn't picking travel by eye: it's seeing what dropper post fits your bike (diameter, insertion and routing) and, once it's mounted, keeping handy what to do if the dropper won't go up.

Common mistake: thinking you pick the travel by taste alone. The one that actually fits is limited by the diameter, the available insertion and the post's stack against your frame, not by the number you like most.
