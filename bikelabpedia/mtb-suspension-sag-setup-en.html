How to set MTB suspension sag

Quick answer

Sag is how much the suspension settles under your riding weight, statically, as a percentage of total travel: the fork ~15-20% and the shock ~25-30%. You measure it like this: push the o-ring all the way down, get on the bike in the attack position, settle without bouncing, get off and measure how far the o-ring moved up; divide it by total travel. It's the foundation of all setup: with sag set, fork air pressure and whether it's too stiff make far more sense. Before touching rebound, compression or tokens, set the sag. It's the starting point for the whole suspension.

What sag is and what to aim for

Sag is the static settle: how much the suspension gives under your riding weight alone, without pedalling or hopping. It's expressed as a percentage of travel. As a starting reference, the fork is usually set to 15-20% and the shock to 25-30%. These are guideline ranges; the rider calibrates within them according to terrain and style.

How to measure it, step by step

1 · O-ring down — Push the o-ring (the rubber seal on the stanchion) down to the lower stop, against the wiper. That way it marks the deepest point the suspension reaches.
2 · Get on in the attack position — Get on the bike in your riding gear. For the fork, stand on the pedals (not seated) in the attack position; for the shock, in the pedalling posture. Lean on a wall or have someone help you.
3 · Settle without bouncing — Set your weight carefully, without bouncing or moving the bike. The o-ring is pushed up the stanchion to where the sag reaches.
4 · Get off and measure — Dismount without touching the o-ring and measure how far it moved up the stanchion from the wiper. That distance is the static settle.
5 · Divide by travel — Sag % = settle ÷ total travel × 100. Compare it with the range (15-20% fork, 25-30% shock) and adjust pressure if needed.

Why it's the foundation of everything

Sag sets the starting point for pressure and suspension behaviour. With correct sag, the next steps —fork pressure by weight, rebound, tokens— make sense. Without it, you tune blind. If the fork feels too stiff, the sag is almost always below the range.

Common mistake: measuring fork sag seated on the saddle, or bouncing on the bike. You have to settle your weight once, without bouncing, and in the right position; otherwise the o-ring moves too far and the sag comes out wrong.
