High vs low speed compression (HSC/LSC)

Quick answer

The first thing almost no one gets right: compression is split by the speed of the suspension shaft, not the speed of the bike. Low speed (LSC) = slow shaft movements (braking dive, cornering loads, pumping, weight transfers): this is what gives support. High speed (HSC) = fast shaft movements (square hits, roots, rocks, landings): this is what controls hard impacts. If the fork feels harsh on square hits, look at HSC; if it blows through travel on long loads, look at LSC.

The mistake that wrecks the most setups is thinking "speed" means the bike's speed. It doesn't: it's how fast the shaft is moving.

Low speed (LSC): the support

Low speed compression acts when the shaft moves slowly. These are the slow inputs: braking dive, cornering loads, terrain pumping and body weight transfers. It's the compression that gives support and keeps the suspension from sinking on every movement.

High speed (HSC): the hard impacts

High speed compression acts when the shaft moves fast. These are the square hits: roots, rocks and landings. It's what controls the hard impacts and harsh bottom-outs without firming up the rest of the travel.

Frequently asked questions

Is the speed the bike's?

No, it's the suspension shaft's. You can be slow and trigger HSC hitting a root, or fast and be in LSC in a corner.

What does LSC control?

Slow shaft movements: braking, cornering loads, pumping and weight transfers. It gives support.

What does HSC control?

Fast shaft movements: square hits, roots, rocks and landings. It controls the hard impacts.
