How much fork travel can I run?

Quick answer

You can usually vary by ±10–20 mm from the stock travel, and many forks let you change it internally (by swapping the air shaft or with spacers). But there are two limits: 1) the maximum travel your frame is rated for (exceeding it risks the warranty and the geometry), and 2) more travel raises the front and slackens the head angle. Never exceed the maker's maximum. First confirm what fork fits your bike.

Adding travel is tempting, but it isn't just "more is better": it changes how the whole bike behaves. Here's the real range and the limits.

The range and the two limits

The usual range. In practice you can vary by around ±10–20 mm from the stock travel. Many forks let you change travel internally: by swapping the air shaft for one of a different length or by adding or removing internal spacers. Not all of them allow it, nor to any value, so check your fork maker's chart.

Limit 1 · The frame rules. The maximum travel the frame maker rates the frame for is the line you must not cross. Going beyond it affects the warranty and the geometry the frame was designed around. Don't exceed the maker's maximum.

Limit 2 · The geometry changes. More travel raises the front of the bike and slackens the head angle. You gain stability descending, but steering gets lazier when climbing and at low speed. It's a change in behaviour, not just comfort.

Key facts

What it pairs with

Before touching travel it helps to know what fork fits your bike and to understand fork offset 44 vs 51, since both affect how it steers. And remember the fork has to be compatible with the rest of the front end, starting with the hub.

Common mistake: running +30 or +40 mm "because the fork allows it" without checking the frame's maximum. Even if the fork goes to that travel, the frame may not be rated for it: you lose the warranty and the bike steers differently from how it was designed.

Frequently asked questions

How much travel can I add or remove?

Usually ±10–20 mm from stock, almost always internally (air shaft or spacers). The limit is the maximum your frame is rated for: don't exceed it.

What happens to the bike if I run more?

The front rises and the head angle slackens: more stable descending, lazier climbing. It's a geometry change, not just comfort.

How is it changed internally?

Depending on the fork, by swapping the air shaft or adding/removing spacers. Check the maker's chart and never exceed the frame's maximum.
