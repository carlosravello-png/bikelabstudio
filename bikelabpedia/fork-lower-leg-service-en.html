Fork lower leg service

Quick answer

The lower leg service is the cheap, frequent maintenance of the fork (RockShox specifies ~50 h; Fox folds it into its interval). The lowers are removed, the bath oil is changed and the seals, dust wipers and foam rings are cleaned or replaced. It restores small-bump sensitivity and removes stiction. It is doable at home with basic tools. See how often each service is due and, if the fork feels harsh, this is usually exactly it.

It is the most accessible maintenance and the most neglected: cheap, frequent and the one that keeps the fork's plushness alive.

What it involves, step by step

1 · Remove the lowers — The lowers are separated from the stanchions. This is the access to everything else: the bath oil, the seals and the foam rings all live in here.
2 · Change the bath oil — The old oil is drained (it comes out dirty, with shavings) and fresh bath oil is added. That oil is what lubricates the stanchions and keeps the slide smooth.
3 · Seals, dust wipers and foam rings — The seals and dust wipers and the foam rings that carry oil to the seals are cleaned or replaced. This is where the sensitivity comes back and the stiction goes away.

Why it matters

The lower leg service is the fork's first line of defense: the bath oil and the foam rings keep the stanchions lubricated. When that oil gets dirty or dries out, stiction appears —the fork stops responding to small bumps and feels harsh and stiff— and the seals start to suffer.

Because it is cheap and frequent, it is the maintenance with the best payoff: you can do it at home with basic tools and it stops wear from reaching the inside, where the repair is far more expensive.

Common mistake: letting the lower leg service hours slide "because the fork still works". By the time it feels harsh and stiff, the dry oil and dry seals are already wearing the stanchions: the cheap fix turns into an expensive one.
