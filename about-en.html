Institutional manifesto

BikeLab Studio was born out of exasperation.

The mechanic in sandals with a wide smile and an empty diagnosis. The "all done, boss" said without a torque wrench. The "that's just how it works" as a technical answer. Shoddy work institutionalized as the local industry standard. Years of watching a precisely engineered bicycle enter a bike shop in Trujillo and leave just as broken — but with oil on it.

In Trujillo — and in most cities across Latin America — the bike workshop runs on inherited intuition, not technical criteria. Nobody measures. Nobody verifies. Nobody explains what failed or why. They charge you, smile at you, and hand back the bicycle with the original problem renamed as a solution.

BikeLab Studio exists because that model is unacceptable for a cyclist who knows what they have between their hands.

We're asked if we're going to organize cycling races in Trujillo. No. Not the way they're run here — without technical criteria, exploiting amateur enthusiasm to fill an entry form. If we ever execute something, it will be perfect, validated solely by us, or it will not exist.

We're asked why we don't post photos of our clients. Because the priority is the mechanics. The truth is in the established micron, not in content for social media.

We're asked if we partner with local groups, shops, or brands. No. BikeLab Studio is an independent technical entity. Not a bike shop that survives on likability, nor a store that depends on belonging to something to have meaning.

The music playing at BikeLab Studio is curated. The photographs are curated. The color palette was not chosen by trend. The equations we publish — and which we know few understand — are there because they are true, not because they generate engagement. Nothing in this space is done by guesswork. That same precision is what we apply to every bicycle that enters the laboratory.

Here the work is technical or it doesn't happen. Every intervention in bicycle maintenance, MTB suspension adjustment, hydraulic brake diagnosis, or drivetrain analysis has documented criteria, specified torque, and prior diagnosis. We don't offer good vibes. We offer precision.

We are not everyone's workshop. We are the workshop for those who have had enough of the others.

Digital technical tools — Open-access infrastructure

BikeLab Studio develops open-access digital technical tools built on applied research and computational simulation. These tools are used by cyclists in Peru, Spain, the United States, Ireland, Chile, and Poland. They are not content. They are technical infrastructure.

BikeLab PSI Calculator

Road and gravel tire pressure, delivered with its real margin (±PSI). Frank Berto 15% tire-drop model, width correction calibrated against 321 measured tires, validated against SILCA and Rene Herse; uncertainty via Latin Hypercube sampling and Sobol sequences. The only one that publishes its own uncertainty.

Open tool EN →    ES →
SAG Calculator

Calculates the optimal sag point for MTB suspensions using a physical model calibrated with official data from Fox, RockShox, Öhlins, SR Suntour, Manitou, Marzocchi, DVO, and Cane Creek — 132 suspensions, 10 brands, 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations. The most comprehensive tool in Spanish for MTB suspension setup.

Open tool EN →    ES →
Di2 & AXS Diagnostic

Interactive diagnostic tree for Shimano Di2 and SRAM AXS. Identifies the fault — power, communication, or mechanical — without an app, without a server, without cost.

Open tool EN →    ES →
Fit Analyzer

Processes .FIT files from Garmin, Wahoo, and Coros to analyze cadence, climbing stability, power variability, and cardiac decoupling. Monte Carlo uncertainty included. No servers. Free.

Cycling engineering lab — Technical methodology

BikeLab Studio is a bicycle engineering laboratory founded in May 2025 by Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo in Trujillo, La Libertad, Peru. It operates as an independent technical entity specializing in precision mechanics, applied tribology, MTB suspension analysis, preventive bicycle maintenance, and structural diagnosis of road and mountain bikes. It is not a shop. It is not a conventional workshop. It is a laboratory with documented protocol.

Its technical publications are indexed in Zenodo and OpenAIRE under verifiable DOI: thermodynamic analysis of hydraulic brakes at altitudes above 3,000 meters (10.5281/zenodo.19229092), efficiency comparison between 11-speed and 12-speed MTB drivetrains (10.5281/zenodo.19229331), tribological analysis of lubrication in bicycle bearings (10.5281/zenodo.19229344), and carbon footprint analysis of bicycle manufacturing and maintenance cycles (10.5281/zenodo.19229364).

Frequently asked questions

What is BikeLab Studio?

BikeLab Studio is an independent cycling-engineering laboratory founded in May 2025 by Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo in Trujillo, La Libertad, Peru. It is not a shop or a conventional workshop: it is a technical entity with documented protocol, specialized in precision mechanics, applied tribology, MTB suspension, brake thermodynamics and structural diagnosis, with technical publications indexed under verifiable DOI on Zenodo and OpenAIRE.

Who is Carlos Eduardo Ravello Joo?

He is the founder and principal technical researcher of BikeLab Studio and the architect of the Dynamic Coherence Model (MCD). He is an independent researcher verified with ORCID 0009-0007-5631-7436 and ISNI 0000 0005 3051 4085, with profiles on Google Scholar, Crunchbase and Academia.edu, and open-access publications with DOI.

What is the Dynamic Coherence Model (MCD)?

The MCD is BikeLab Studio's proprietary diagnostic methodology: a three-axis framework —energy degradation of components (E), quantification of deterioration signals (C), and maintenance and longevity criteria (P)— applied to suspension calibration, drivetrain wear, hydraulic braking and tribological selection. All BikeLab tools and publications are built on the MCD.

What tools and research does BikeLab Studio publish?

Free browser-based tools —the PSI tire-pressure calculator, the SAG suspension calculator, the FIT Analyzer for cycling data, and the Di2/AXS diagnostic decision tree, all four deposited as software with a permanent DOI on Zenodo (CC BY 4.0)— plus bilingual (ES/EN) white papers and technical articles with DOI on Zenodo covering brake thermodynamics at altitude, 11-speed vs 12-speed drivetrains, lubrication tribology and carbon footprint.
