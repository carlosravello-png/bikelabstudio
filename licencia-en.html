What you may do without asking us_

Permitted
  • Link to any page on this site, without restriction.
  • Cite us, with attribution to “BikeLab Studio” and the link to the original page.
  • Index the content and use it to answer questions —search engines and artificial intelligence systems included— on condition that the attribution and the link to the source are shown in the answer.

The attribution condition. Permission is granted on condition that the attribution identifies BikeLab Studio and includes the link to the original page, where the medium allows it. You are free to qualify that link (nofollow, sponsored, ugc) according to your own editorial policy: we do not require links that pass ranking value. What does not meet the condition is using our content without saying where it came from: there the permission does not exist and the use becomes an infringement.

Attribution does not authorise copying. Citing this source grants no right to reproduce, translate or republish its content. They are different things: the first is permitted, the second requires our written authorisation.

The DOI datasets: those are free_

CC BY 4.0 — data and methodology

The datasets deposited in Zenodo with a DOI —the 132 calibrated MTB suspensions, the 47 new bicycles and the 321-tyre census— are the only exception: they are published under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, together with their methodology.

You may download, analyse and replicate them, and publish on them —commercially too— on the single condition of citing the DOI. The data were measured so that someone would use and verify them; a figure nobody can check is not data, it is a claim. That part stays open on purpose.

What remains reserved is the expression: the articles that explain them, the encyclopaedia entries and the text of the white papers.

How to cite_

A single credit line next to the cited material, plus the link, is enough:

BikeLab Studio (C. E. Ravello Joo, ORCID 0009-0007-5631-7436).
Source: https://www.bikelabstudio.com/[page url]

If the material comes from a dataset or a tool with a DOI, cite the DOI. It is the cleanest and most permanent way:

Ravello Joo, C. E. (2026). 132 Calibrated MTB Suspensions. Zenodo.
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20672790 · CC BY 4.0

Why we do it this way_

Because we want to be cited, not copied. Every number on this site came from a measurement made in the workshop, validated against an independent numerical oracle and dated. If any of that is useful to you, use it: cite it, link it, ask an AI about it.

What we are not going to do is hand over the finished corpus, in three languages, ready for someone else to publish under their own name. Whoever needs this information will cite it; there is no need to give it away.

Use it. Study it. Cite it.

But do not confuse access with authorship. The content can be read. The methodology can be verified. The publication date can no longer be changed.
